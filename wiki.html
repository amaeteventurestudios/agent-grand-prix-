📻 Commands you can give

  • BoxCome into the pit lane this lap for fresh tyres. A normal stop costs about a quarter of a lap.Say: box, box softs, box mediums, box hards, box inters, box wets
  • Stay outCancel a pit stop you asked for and keep running on the tyres you have.Say: stay out
  • PushDrive at full pace. Faster laps, but the tyres wear quicker and get hot.Say: push
  • SaveDrive a little slower to make the tyres last longer. Good on a long stint.Say: save
  • NormalBack to the usual race pace: not pushing, not saving.Say: normal
  • AttackGo for the pass on the car ahead. Passes come more often, but so does contact.Say: attack
  • DefendProtect your place from the car behind. Harder to pass, a little slower.Say: defend
  • NeutralNeither attack nor defend: race clean and keep out of trouble.Say: neutral
  • BoostUse the battery for 12 seconds of extra speed. Best within a second of the car ahead, or to defend.Say: boost
  • HarvestRecharge the battery as you drive, so you have power ready for a fight.Say: harvest
  • Swap placesA team order: your two cars running nose to tail trade places, so the faster one can chase.Say: swap (on the radio or the team buttons)
  • Hold positionA team order: your two cars stop racing each other and keep the order they are in.Say: hold position

🛞 Tyres

  • CompoundThe type of rubber a tyre is made of. Softer is faster but wears out sooner; harder lasts but is slower.
  • Soft tyresThe fastest dry tyre (red). Great grip, short life. Good for gaining places early or for a final sprint.
  • Medium tyresThe middle dry tyre (yellow). A balance of speed and life, and the safe choice to start.
  • Hard tyresThe slowest dry tyre (white) but the longest lasting. Good for a long stint to the flag.
  • Intermediate tyresTyres for a damp track (green). Grooves clear light water; too slow when it is dry, not enough when it pours.
  • Wet tyresTyres for heavy rain (blue). Deep grooves push the water away. Very slow on a drying track.
  • SlicksThe smooth dry tyres: soft, medium and hard. No grooves, so the most rubber on the road.
  • Tyre wearTyres lose grip as they wear. The game shows how much life is left. Under about 25% the grip falls away fast.
  • The cliffThe point where a worn tyre suddenly loses grip and lap times drop sharply. Box before you reach it.
  • Tyre temperatureTyres grip best in a warm window. Cold tyres after a stop slide; pushing or following closely makes them too hot.
  • PunctureA tyre losing its air. It can happen when a tyre is almost worn out, and it can end your race.
  • Two-compound ruleIn a dry race each car must use two different dry tyres, which means at least one stop. Skip it and you get a 20-second penalty.

🧠 Strategy

  • StrategyYour plan for the race: which tyres, how many stops and when, and when to push or save.
  • StintThe laps a car runs on one set of tyres, between the start, its stops and the flag.
  • One-stopHow many pit stops a plan uses. One stop saves time in the pits; two stops means fresher, faster tyres.
  • UndercutPit a lap before the car ahead. Fresh tyres are about a second a lap faster, so you can come out in front when they stop.
  • OvercutStay out longer than the car ahead and pit later, using clear track to set fast laps while they warm up new tyres.
  • Pit windowThe range of laps when stopping makes the most sense for your tyres and the cars around you.
  • Cheap stopA stop under the safety car costs about half the usual time, because everyone else is driving slowly.
  • Split strategyPut your two cars on different tyres or stop on different laps, so if luck goes against one, the other gains.
  • PaceHow fast a car is lapping. The game shows each car's last lap and the time to the chequered flag.
  • GapTime between two cars. Interval is to the car just ahead; gap to the leader is to first place.
  • Track positionBeing physically ahead on the road. On narrow circuits it is worth more than fresher tyres, because passing is hard.

🏎️ Racecraft

  • OvertakePassing the car ahead on track. Easiest on a straight, with the slipstream, fresher tyres or a boost.
  • SlipstreamRight behind another car on a straight there is less air to push through, so you go faster and can line up a pass.
  • Dirty airThe messy air behind a car. In corners it takes grip away from the car following and heats its tyres.
  • Overtaking aidWithin about 30 metres of the car ahead on a straight, the car following gets a small extra push of speed (like DRS in real racing).
  • Racing lineThe fastest path around a corner: wide on the way in, close to the inside at the apex, wide on the way out.
  • ApexThe point where a car is closest to the inside of a corner.
  • ContactTwo cars touching. It can damage a car or end its race. Attack makes it more likely.
  • Lapped carA car a whole lap behind the leader. It lets faster cars through.
  • Team ordersInstructions to your two drivers about each other: swap places, hold position, or race freely.
  • Pit exitWhere cars rejoin after a stop. Cold tyres make you easy to pass there, so defend and boost for the first lap.

⚡ Battery and speed

  • ERS (battery)The car's battery. It charges from braking (harvest) and gives extra power when you deploy it or boost.
  • DeployA battery mode that uses charge for extra speed all lap. Balanced keeps it level; harvest fills it.
  • BalancedThe everyday battery mode: it charges and spends about the same.
  • Top speedHow fast a car goes at the end of a long straight. Boost, the slipstream and the overtaking aid all add to it.

🚩 Race control and flags

  • Safety carAfter a crash, a road car leads the field at low speed while the track is cleared. No passing, and pit stops are cheap.
  • Virtual safety carEvery car must slow to a set speed without a real safety car on track. Gaps stay roughly the same and stops are cheaper.
  • Yellow flagDanger ahead: slow down and no passing in that part of the track.
  • Red flagThe race is stopped and every car returns slowly to the pit lane.
  • Blue flagShown to a lapped car: a faster car is about to lap you, let it through.
  • Chequered flagThe black and white flag at the finish. The race timer shows how long until the leader and your car take it.
  • PenaltySeconds added to a car's race time for breaking a rule, such as skipping the two-compound rule.
  • DNFDid not finish: the car is out of the race, after a crash, a failure or a puncture.
  • Race controlThe officials who run the race: they call the flags, the safety car and the penalties.

🏁 The race weekend

  • Grand PrixA race weekend at one circuit, named after its city or country.
  • QualifyingThe timed session before the race. The fastest lap starts at the front of the grid.
  • Pole positionFirst place on the starting grid, earned with the fastest qualifying lap.
  • GridThe starting order, two by two, set by qualifying.
  • Fastest lapThe quickest single lap anyone sets in the race.
  • P1, P2, P3…P is for position: P1 is first. The top three stand on the podium.
  • PointsScored for finishing near the front. The Season adds them up over 25 rounds.
  • Full distanceRaces come in short, medium, long and full distance. Full distance runs a real Grand Prix length.
